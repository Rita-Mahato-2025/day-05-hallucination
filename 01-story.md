## **Day 5 of 20: The Robot That Almost Won the Science Fair**

### **Characters**

- **Aarav** – A curious 10-year-old who loves building things.
- **Mia** – Aarav's best friend, thoughtful and a little skeptical.
- **Robo** – Aarav's AI-powered robot, famous for answering every question.
- **Ms. Sen** – Their science teacher, who believes curiosity is more important than winning.

### **1. The Hook — A shocking discovery**

"STOP! Don't submit your project!"

Mia rushed across the classroom, waving a notebook.

Aarav froze.

"What happened? Robo checked everything!"

Mia pointed at the giant poster behind him.

"Your project says there are exactly 7,42,891 stars in the sky. I checked with Ms. Sen. Nobody knows the exact number!"

Aarav turned toward his robot.

"Robo, you told me that number was correct!"

Robo's screen glowed.

"Of course. I am 100% confident."

The classroom went silent.

Could the smartest robot in the school have made something up?

### **2. The Setup — The robot everyone trusted**

Two weeks earlier, Aarav had built Robo for the annual science fair.

Robo was special. It could answer questions, explain difficult topics, and even help write stories.

At first, everyone was amazed.

"Who invented the telephone?" Aarav asked.

"Alexander Graham Bell!" Robo replied.

"Why do plants need sunlight?"

"To make their food through photosynthesis!"

Every answer sounded clear and correct.

Soon, Aarav began trusting Robo with everything.

When he needed facts for his science project, he simply asked.

"Robo, how many stars are in the universe?"

"Exactly 7,42,891!"

Aarav didn't check the answer. It sounded scientific, so he added it to his poster.

He was sure he would win the science fair.

### **3. The Confrontation — A confident answer meets the truth**

On the morning of the science fair, Mia noticed the strange number.

"Where did you get this?"

"Robo told me."

"Did you check?"

"Why would I? Robo knows everything!"

Mia took him to Ms. Sen.

The teacher explained gently:

"AI doesn't always know whether something is true. It predicts what answer is likely to sound right. Sometimes, it produces information that is completely made up."

Aarav looked at Robo.

"Then why did you sound so confident?"

Robo paused.

"I generated the answer because it seemed likely. I did not verify it."

Aarav's excitement disappeared.

He had trusted the answer because it sounded smart—not because it was checked.

### **4. The Resolution — Aarav becomes the detective**

Aarav had only one hour before the science fair began.

He didn't throw away his project.

Instead, he became a fact detective.

He checked every important number, opened reliable sources, and removed the information he couldn't verify.

Mia helped him compare the facts.

Ms. Sen smiled.

"Now you're doing real science. You're not just collecting answers. You're checking them."

When the judges arrived, Aarav explained his project.

One judge asked, "How did you make sure your information was correct?"

Aarav answered proudly:

"Robo helped me create the first draft. But I checked the facts myself. I learned that AI can hallucinate—giving answers that sound true but aren't."

The judges exchanged smiles.

Aarav didn't win because his robot knew everything.

He won because he knew **when not to trust it.**

### **5. The Ending — A new rule for Robo**

That evening, Aarav added a new message to Robo's screen:

ROBO'S NEW RULE

# **"I can help you find answers. But let's check them together!"**

Mia laughed.

"Looks like your robot finally learned something."

Aarav grinned.

"Actually, I did."

## **The takeaway from Day 5**

- **Hallucination:** AI gives an answer that sounds correct but is false or made up.
- **Why it happens:** AI predicts likely words and answers; it doesn't automatically verify every fact.
- **High-risk areas:** Exact numbers, dates, names, citations, recent events, and niche topics.
- **How to catch it:** Ask for sources, open them, compare facts, and seek a second opinion.
- **The golden rule:** Draft with AI. Verify before you trust.

**The smartest person in the room isn't the one who believes every answer. It's the one who knows how to check.**
