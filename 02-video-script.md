## ** Day 5 of 20: The Robot That Almost Won the Science Fair **

### **Format: Animated or illustrated story, voiceover with character voices**
### **Length: About 90 seconds**

### **SCENE 1: The Hook (0:00–0:10)**

Visual: Classroom. Mia runs in, waving a notebook. Aarav freezes next to his poster.
On-screen text: "7,42,891 stars?"

MIA: Stop! Don't submit your project!
AARAV: What happened? Robo checked everything!
MIA: Your poster says there are exactly 7,42,891 stars. Ms. Sen says nobody knows the exact number!
AARAV: Robo! You told me it was correct!
ROBO: Of course. I am 100% confident.

NARRATOR: Could the smartest robot in school have made it up?

### **SCENE 2: The Setup (0:10–0:25)**

Visual: Flashback. Aarav building Robo. Robo's screen glows as it answers questions.

NARRATOR: Two weeks earlier, Aarav built Robo for the science fair. It answered everything.

AARAV: Who invented the telephone?
ROBO: Alexander Graham Bell!
AARAV: Why do plants need sunlight?
ROBO: To make food through photosynthesis!

NARRATOR: Every answer sounded right. So Aarav stopped checking.

AARAV: Robo, how many stars are in the universe?
ROBO: Exactly 7,42,891!

Visual: Aarav writes it on the poster without a second thought.

### **SCENE 3: The Confrontation (0:25–0:45)**

Visual: Mia and Aarav in front of Ms. Sen. Robo's screen flickers.

MIA: Did you check it?
AARAV: Why would I? Robo knows everything!

MS. SEN (gently): AI doesn't always know what's true. It predicts what answer sounds right. Sometimes, it makes things up.

AARAV: Then why did you sound so sure?
ROBO (pauses): I generated the answer because it seemed likely. I did not verify it.

On-screen text: Sounded smart ≠ Was checked

### **SCENE 4: The Resolution (0:45–1:10)**

Visual: Clock ticking, one hour left. Aarav and Mia flip through books and open websites. Aarav crosses out the unverified facts.

NARRATOR: Aarav had one hour. He didn't give up. He became a fact detective.

MS. SEN (smiling): Now you're doing real science. You're not collecting answers. You're checking them.

Visual: Science fair. A judge leans in.

JUDGE: How did you make sure your information was correct?
AARAV: Robo helped with the first draft. But I checked the facts myself. AI can hallucinate, which means giving answers that sound true but aren't.

NARRATOR: Aarav didn't win because his robot knew everything. He won because he knew when not to trust it.

### **SCENE 5: The Ending (1:10–1:20)**

Visual: Evening. Aarav types a new message on Robo's screen.
On-screen text: ROBO'S NEW RULE: "I can help you find answers. But let's check them together!"

MIA (laughing): Looks like your robot finally learned something.
AARAV (grinning): Actually, I did.

### **SCENE 6: The Takeaway (1:20–1:30)**

Visual: Quick text cards, one per beat, with a voiceover from you.

RITA (voiceover): Day 5 takeaway. Hallucination is when AI gives an answer that sounds right but is made up. It happens because AI predicts likely words. It doesn't verify facts. Be extra careful with exact numbers, dates, names, citations and recent events. Ask for sources, open them, compare, get a second opinion.

On-screen text (final card):
Draft with AI. Verify before you trust.

RITA: The smartest person in the room isn't the one who believes every answer. It's the one who knows how to check. See you on Day 6!

### **Production notes**
**Voices:** Use a bright, confident voice for Robo, with a small pause before "I did not verify it."
**Visual gag:** Show "100% confident" on Robo's screen in Scene 1, then cross it out in Scene 3.
**Engagement hook for the caption:** "Has AI ever given you a confident answer that turned out to be wrong? Tell me in the comments!"
